4.2 State Precedence Rules
1. A pillar MAY mutate only state within its jurisdiction.
2. No pillar MAY overwrite another pillar's authoritative state directly.
3. Cross-pillar changes occur through registered events and versioned references.
4. An MDGR verdict MAY constrain effective execution without rewriting historical AI Founder OS 
records.
5. An EASGF authorization failure MAY prevent AI/tool activity without changing business intent.
6. A business cancellation MAY terminate a workflow even when a prior governance verdict was 
permissive.
7. Revocation creates a new event/state version; historical decisions remain immutable.
8. Effective execution is recomputed from current authoritative state, not narrative recency.

If any mandatory predicate is FALSE or UNRESOLVED, consequential execution MUST be denied, held, or

10. Core Registries, LKG Snapshots & Material Policy Mutation
10.1 Identity & Authority Registry (IAR)
IAR is the canonical source for identity status, roles, delegated authority, signing/transaction limits, 
separation of duties, temporary delegation, vetoes, conflicts of interest, and expiry. Authority claims from 
conversational text are non-authoritative until resolved against IAR or an approved equivalent source.
10.2 Evidence & Provenance Service (EPS)
EVIDENCE DISTINCTION. Source Authorization ≠ Source Authenticity ≠ Source Authority ≠ Source Freshness 
≠ Substantive Truth.
10.3 Policy & Constraint Registry (PCR)
PCR MUST distinguish at least MANDATORY, CONDITIONAL, ADVISORY, and NON_DEROGABLE constraints 
and identify whether each constraint is machine-evaluable or requires authorized human interpretation.
10.4 Pinned Last-Known-Good Snapshot
▪ LKG snapshots MUST be signed/pinned, versioned, and associated with an explicit TTL.
▪ LKG use is prohibited after TTL expiry.
▪ LKG MUST NOT override a known revocation or newer blocking event.
▪ Authority classes requiring live verification MUST NOT fall back to LKG unless policy explicitly permits 
it.
▪ Every degraded-mode use of LKG MUST emit an audit event.
▪ LKG use MAY support bounded read/advisory or policy-defined continuity; it MUST NOT silently 
expand execution authority.
[Source page 15 of 33]
Each signed LKG snapshot MUST identify its signing authority or approved configuration/policy authority, 
signature or integrity mechanism, trust-anchor reference, issuance time, version, and TTL.
Verification of an LKG snapshot MUST terminate in a trusted key, certificate, attestation root, or 
equivalent trust anchor maintained outside transient model context. The consuming model or 
probabilistic reasoning component MUST NOT create, replace, or self-assert the trust anchor used to 
validate the snapshot.
If the applicable trust anchor cannot be resolved or validated, the LKG snapshot MUST be treated as 
untrusted and MUST NOT restore material decision or consequential execution eligibility.
10.5 Material Policy Mutation Governance
MATERIAL POLICY MUTATION RULE. Changes to constraint derogability, NON_DEROGABLE classification, 
materiality thresholds, authority thresholds, or other controls that can materially weaken the governance 
perimeter MUST themselves be treated as material governance actions. Such changes require jurisdiction￾valid authority, auditable approval, and the policy-defined separation-of-duties or dual-authorization 
controls applicable to their risk class.
Downgrading or removing a NON_DEROGABLE constraint requires an explicitly authorized meta￾governance process and MUST NOT be achievable solely through ordinary PCR editing rights.
A mutation governed by this section is a policy-layer material action: it MUST route through MDGR triage 
plus jurisdiction-valid human authority, MUST emit a governance event, and MUST trigger re-entry 
evaluation for affected active verdicts and tokens under §8.1. This section governs PCR content mutation; 
it is distinct from, and additional to, the specification change-control procedure in §19, which governs the 
AFGS text itself.
11. Governed Execution Gateway, PECG/PESR & Revocation
11.1 Controlled Egress Enforcement Boundary
GEG is the deployment-controlled egress boundary through which consequential external mutations 
MUST pass when SC3 behavior is claimed. The gateway cannot guarantee atomicity inside third-party 
systems; it governs the pre-egress decision and the execution attempt within the deployment's control 
boundary.
11.2 PECG Composition
PECG — Pre-Execution Consistency Guard
 +-- PESR: current state / policy / authority / condition revalidation
 +-- execution lease or short-lived reservation
 +-- revocation check: push buffer + authoritative pull
[Source page 16 of 33]
 +-- idempotency-key enforcement
 +-- replay prevention / single-use nonce
 +-- scope match
 +-- human approval check if REQUIRED
 +-- compensation / reconciliation semantics
 |
 +-- PASS -> controlled egress attempt
 +-- FAIL -> DENY / HOLD / ESCALATE
11.3 Distributed Execution Semantics
NO ABSOLUTE CROSS-SYSTEM ATOMICITY CLAIM. AFGS requires the strongest available consistency 
boundary using state-version checks, short-lived leases/reservations, idempotency keys, replay prevention, 
and explicit compensation/reconciliation where external atomicity cannot be guaranteed.

revocation_policy:

No universal revocation latency is hard-coded. Deployments MUST publish and test their configured 
propagation SLO, maximum staleness, acknowledgement behavior, and fail-closed threshold.
11.6 PESR Authoritative Read Consistency
AUTHORITATIVE READ RULE. PESR state and revocation checks MUST be served by the authoritative system 
of record or by a replica whose maximum observed lag is bounded within the configured 
[Source page 17 of 33]
maximum_staleness_ms. If that freshness bound cannot be established, execution MUST fail closed.
This rule closes the window between revocation commit and revocation visibility at the enforcement 
point: the push buffer of §11.5 is defense-in-depth, and the authoritative pull is the safety-bearing check. 
An implementation whose PESR reads an unbounded-lag replica does not satisfy GS-I7.
11.7 Trusted Time & Freshness Authority
TRUSTED TIME RULE. Expiry, TTL, freshness, and validity-window decisions at PECG/PESR and LKG 
verification MUST use a trusted and synchronized time source. If time integrity cannot be established, 
consequential execution MUST fail closed.
This rule applies to execution-token TTL (§11.4), verdict validity windows (§8.2, §9.2), LKG TTL (§10.4), 
break-glass TTL (§12.2), and revocation staleness bounds (§11.5). The time source is part of the assurance 
surface: its identity, synchronization mechanism, and failure behavior MUST be documented in the 
deployment's conformance evidence. GS-I7 freshness guarantees are only as strong as the clock against 
which they are evaluated.

21. Post-Freeze Implementation Roadmap
1. Materiality Policy & Canonical State Machine — production-grade policy objects, legal transitions, 
linked-decision scope, and state invariants.
2. Formal JSON Schema / Protobuf Contracts — machine-validated Stack Envelope, Verdict, Token, 
IAR/EPS/PCR objects, Override, Revocation, and Error Envelopes.
3. SC2 Reference Implementation — external validator middleware, registries, and state engine.
4. Conformance Test Suite & Adversarial Benchmark — ground-truth fixtures, scope-splitting, authority 
spoofing, stale policy/evidence, revocation, bypass, and validator-circularity tests (closes KL-R5).
5. SC3 Enforcement Implementation — GEG, PECG/PESR, token/revocation services, controlled egress, 
resilience, idempotency, reconciliation, and trusted-time infrastructure per §11.7.
6. Empirical Evaluation — measured false allow/block, materiality precision/recall, latency, cost, human
burden, and utility loss.
7. External Validation / Independent Certification, if established — separate assurance milestone; not 
implied by conformance alone.
